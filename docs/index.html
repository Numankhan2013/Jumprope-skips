How to film it
  • Prop the phone up so it stays put.
  • Stand close enough to fill at least half the height of the picture. Keep your head and shoulders in frame.
  • Be the only person moving in the shot.
  • Any place works: grass, concrete, indoors. Sound isn't used.

How this is worked out
  • Intensity comes from your measured pace: rope jumping is 8.8 MET under 100 skips/min, 11.8 at 100–120, and 12.3 at 120–160 (Compendium of Physical Activities).
  • One MET is set to your resting metabolism from weight, height, age and sex (Mifflin-St Jeor), not a textbook average.
  • Rest time between sets counts at 2 MET. Any estimate like this is about ±15% for one person, hence the range.

      Already know your count? Get calories without a video

      How it counts. It follows how you move up and down from frame to frame, finds the rhythm of your jumps, and counts one skip per bounce. Pauses, the swing that starts a set, the hop where the rope catches your feet, and moments when the phone moves are left out.

      Analysis runs in your browser and takes roughly a third to half of the video's length. Keep the screen on while it works.
